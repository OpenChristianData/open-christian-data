# Open Christian Data v0.2.0

**Release date:** 2026-07-17
**Previous release:** `v0.1.0` (2026-04-12)
**Dataset:** [OpenChristianDataOrg/open-christian-data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenChristianDataOrg/open-christian-data)

Open Christian Data aims to bring all freely available public-domain Christian
texts into one unified collection. Much of that literature survives in old
editions, archives, websites, and specialist formats, but not in a form that can
be readily searched, compared, or used.

`v0.2.0` brings the project much closer to filling that gap. It is a
substantially larger collection, gives a clearer account of the people, works,
editions, and sources represented in it, and corrects problems that could have
misled downstream users.

**Nearly 150 million words of Christian texts across 12 categories, including
363 books.** The collection also includes 34,904 hymns, 5,297 sermons, nine
Bible translations, and extensive commentary and reference material.

## A much larger library

The first release was a small foundation. `v0.2.0` turns it into a substantial
library spanning Christian theology, biblical interpretation, worship,
formation, preaching, history, and reference.

The largest areas of growth are:

- Bible coverage grew from one translation to nine.
- Structured literature grew from 11 named works to 288 works, adding far more
  theology, church history, devotional writing, and other public-domain books.
- Sermon coverage grew from one 36-sermon collection to seven works containing
  5,297 sermons.
- Hymns are now part of the published dataset, with a collection of 34,904
  entries.
- Commentary coverage now includes 34 works and 115,069 entries.

The collection includes Matthew Henry's *Exposition of the Old and New
Testament*, Spurgeon's *Metropolitan Tabernacle Pulpit*, the *Catholic
Encyclopedia*, *Nave's Topical Bible*, Augustine's *City of God*, Calvin's
*Institutes of the Christian Religion*, and editions of the *Book of Common
Prayer*.

The point of this growth is not simply to produce more rows. It brings more of
the Christian tradition into an open collection where each passage remains
connected to an author, a work, and a source.

## A clearer account of each work

The schemas have been revised as the collection has grown. Contributors are
now represented as people with roles, rather than as names in a list. Editions,
source history, and provenance are recorded more clearly. The structures used
for Bible texts, catechisms, church fathers, devotionals, doctrinal documents,
prayers, long-form works, and reference material have also been strengthened.

These changes matter because Christian texts are not interchangeable pieces of
content. Authorship, translation, edition, historical setting, and source all
shape how a work should be read and used. `v0.2.0` preserves more of that
context and makes it easier to trace a record back to the evidence behind it.

## More trustworthy texts and metadata

This release includes a broad correction pass across the collection. Two
corrections are worth naming, because each changed what users receive.

**Bible-book routing in the SWORD commentary imports was repaired.** The fix
restored **1,166 commentary entries** that had been omitted entirely: 115 from
Barnes, 485 from Calvin, and 566 from Wesley. It also corrected where commentary
material was placed in the biblical structure, so entries now sit against the
passages they actually discuss.

**Five doctrinal records were removed on rights grounds.** A source audit found
that `christ-hymn-of-colossians`, `christ-hymn-of-philippians`, `christian-shema`,
`confession-of-peter`, and `shema-yisrael` carried text supplied by the upstream
Creeds.json project from a copyrighted modern Bible translation, while being
labeled CC0. Each is a bare Scripture passage rather than a composed creed, so
the wording is wholly that translation's and no public-domain text sits
underneath to recover. They were removed rather than relabeled. No text is lost
to the collection: every one of those passages remains available in the CC0
Berean Standard Bible under `bible_text`. What is lost is the editorial
identification of these passages as creedal fragments, which can be restored
once the wording is re-sourced from a public-domain translation.

The work catalog and public counts are now generated from the collection
instead of maintained by hand. Structural validation, source comparison, and
provenance make errors easier to find and correct.

## Collection at a glance

| Category | Configuration | What the collection contains |
|---|---|---|
| Books and long-form works | `structured_text` | 288 works, including theology, church history, treatises, and devotional classics, divided into 259,198 passages for publication |
| Bible translations | `bible_text` | 9 translations containing 282,395 verses |
| Commentaries | `commentary` | 34 commentaries containing 115,069 entries |
| Dictionaries and encyclopedias | `reference_entry` | 6 dictionaries and encyclopedias containing 25,682 entries |
| Topical Bibles and indexes | `topical_reference` | *Nave's Topical Bible* and *Torrey's New Topical Textbook*, containing 5,945 topics |
| Devotionals | `devotional` | 2 devotionals containing 1,464 readings |
| Sermons | `sermon` | 5,297 sermons from 7 collections |
| Creeds, confessions, and doctrinal documents | `doctrinal_document` | 33 documents published as 1,314 articles and clauses |
| Catechisms | `catechism_qa` | 15 catechisms containing 3,509 questions and answers |
| Prayers and liturgies | `prayer` | 4 sources containing 205 prayers and collects |
| Hymns | `hymn_collection` | 34,904 hymn texts |
| Church Fathers quotations | `church_fathers` | 70,164 scripture-linked quotations |

Long-form works and doctrinal documents are divided into passages when
published on Hugging Face, producing 805,146 downloadable records across 12
configurations. That is a technical loading statistic, not the best measure of
the library itself.

`v0.1.0` contained 247,649 rows across 11 configurations. It did not yet keep a
comparable work-level catalog, so an exact before-and-after count of works is
not available.

## What users need to know

The Hugging Face repository ID has not changed. All 11 configurations from
`v0.1.0` remain available, and `hymn_collection` is the new twelfth
configuration.

Three things will affect code written against `v0.1.0`:

- **Row counts changed everywhere.** Any code that hardcodes expected row counts
  needs updating.
- **Five `doctrinal_document` records were removed** (listed above). Code that
  pins those five `_source_id` values will find them absent.
- **`summary` and `key_quote` remain empty.** Both fields ship `null` with
  `summary_review_status` set to `"withheld"`. They are published empty rather
  than filled with unreviewed generated text.

Once the Hub tags are created, this release can be pinned explicitly:

```python
from datasets import load_dataset

commentary = load_dataset(
    "OpenChristianDataOrg/open-christian-data",
    "commentary",
    revision="v0.2.0",
)
```

`main` continues to point to the latest published version. For exact
reproduction, use either a release tag or an immutable Hub commit.

## Known limitations

This remains an English-language historical collection shaped by the
availability of usable public-domain editions. Coverage is uneven across
periods, traditions, regions, and authors. Historical works also preserve the
language and assumptions of their time; inclusion is not endorsement.

Validation catches malformed data, not every textual or editorial mistake.
Source transcriptions, dates, labels, and edition information may still need
correction. The dataset should not be treated as a doctrinal authority or as a
critical edition of every work it contains.

Three rights questions remain open and are recorded in the
[source ledger](../SOURCES.md) rather than treated as settled: the license
covering the JWBickel structured data, the Wikisource transcription layer, and a
translation warning carried by the HistoricalChristianFaith source. Where a
question cannot be resolved, the affected records are removed or replaced rather
than relabeled.
