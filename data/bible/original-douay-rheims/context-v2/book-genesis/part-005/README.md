#### Annotation 12

**Head**

Let it not.

**Annotation Text**

\[1\] It seemed so hard, inhuman, and unjust to cast Agar and Ismael out of the house, that Abraham would not have done it, unless God had commanded him in this to hear the voice of Sara. But when he knew God's will therein, not respecting flesh and blood, nor disputing further of the lawfulness of the fact, sent them both away, commending them to God's protection: and so by this separation the family was made quiet. \[2\] It represented also a notable Mystery of the Synagogue of the Jews and Church of Christ. As S. Augustine \[3\] doth excellently expound the same, according to S. Paul (Gal. 4.) That Ismael's playing with Isaac was persecution, because it tended to pervert him, shewing it to be a greater injury to delude and deceive any, in drawing them to new and particular companies, than corporally to persecute them: \[4\] and that the Church and Catholic Princes punish heretics for their good \[5\], to make them return to the truth, or finally to cease from seducing others. \[6\] *Ecce libera afflixit ancillam, &amp; non illam vocat persecutionem. Apostolus, ludit servus cum domino, &amp; persecutionem vocat. Afflictio ista non vocatur persecutio, &amp; lusio illa vocatur persecutio.* Behold the freewoman afflicted the handmaid, and the Apostle calleth it not persecution, the servant playeth with the Master, and he calleth it persecution \[7\]. This affliction is not called persecution, and that play is called persecution. Again \[8\], he saith of Sara, *Illusionem vidit in illo lusu, animadvertit servi superbiam, displicuit illi, eiecit foras.* She saw foul play in that play, she noted the servant's pride, it displeased her, she cast him forth of the doors. S. Hierom \[9\] also teacheth that the spiritual never persecuteth the carnal: but spareth him as his rustical brother, knowing that he may in time be profitable.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 14

**Head**

The maid to whom I shall say.

**Annotation Text**

Observation of speeches called ominous, which are interpreted to signify good or evil luck, \[1\] are sometimes superstitious &amp; suggested by evil spirits, who now &amp; then telling or insinuating some truth, get credit, &amp; so allure men to attend to such vain, uncertain, and unlawful signs, as S. Augustine testifieth (li. 2. de Gen. ad lit. c. 17. &amp; li. 12. c. 22.) \[2\] Nevertheless such signs are sometimes lawfully observed, &amp; desired from God, as the same Doctor disputeth (li. quest. super Gen. q. 53) &amp; S. Chrysostom teacheth more clearly (ho. 45. in Gen.) likewise S. Theodoret (q. 73. in Gen.) But whosoever will not err in particular cases, must follow either express Scripture, or the judgement of the Church \[3\] \[4\], which is always directed by the spirit of truth. And touching this prayer of Abraham's servant, &amp; his desire of this determinate sign to know the maid, whom God had provided to be Isaac's wife, the fathers generally hold that it was religious, devout, and discreet \[5\]. For he being careful of his Master's business, and not trusting his own judgement, but relying upon that Abraham had said, *Our Lord shall send his Angel before thee*, commended so weighty a cause to God by prayer, the Angel suggesting both to him to desire, &amp; to the maid to perform (as the event sheweth) such qualities and virtues in her, as were most agreeable to the great charity and hospitality daily practised in Abraham's house, most convenient and necessary (as he discreetly considered) for that family, and good of many. The like observations were approved in Gedeon \[6\], and Ionathas \[7\]. And to pray for such signs in some case, or for manifest miracles is also approved by the Apostle's example praying God *To shew by lot which of the two he had chosen to the Apostleship, in place of Judas.* \[8\] And that he would extend his hand to cures, and signs, and wonders, to be done by the name of his holy son 〔small caps: Jesus〕.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 6

**Head**

To the children of his concubines.

**Annotation Text**

\[1\] S. Augustin (li. 16. c. 34. de civit.) sheweth that both Agar and Cetura, being Abraham's lawful wives (for so they are called in holy Scripture) are also called concubines, because they had not like privileges to Sara, whose son was sole heir to his father, and the children of the others had only gifts (or moveable goods) not attaining to the promised Kingdom. And all this for mystery sake. For Ismael signified the carnal people before Christ, the children of Cetura prefigured Heretics \[2\], who suppose themselves to pertain to the new Testament, but are separated no less than the Jews from Christ's Kingdom. And albeit there was also another particular reason why Agar was called concubine, because she was a second wife the first then living, yet this learned father saith, he did not see why Cetura, being married after the death of Sara, should be called concubine, but only for this Mystery.

#### Annotation 21

**Head**

Heard him.

**Annotation Text**

Notwithstanding God's assured promise, that Isaac should have issue (Gen. 21. v. 12.) yet he prayeth instantly for the same. And Moyses here attributeth Rebecca's conceiving to Isaac's prayer. ◦ Whereby we see that God's foreseeing, predestinating, and promising, exclude not, but indeed include secondary causes, and ordinary means, by which his eternal will and pleasure is fulfilled. For as God did foresee that Rebecca should have children, so he did foresee that Isaac should pray for it, and obtain it; and the one was as sure to come to pass as the other. And the same consequence is true concerning eternal life as S. Gregory teacheth li. 1. c. 8. Dialog.

God's predestination &amp; foreseeing include, &amp; not exclude the means by which his will is done.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 19

**Head**

I am thy first-begotten Esau.

**Annotation Text**

Jacob was not by nature the first-begotten, but by God's ordinance, &amp; by covenant made with Esau ◦, had right to the preeminence and privileges belonging to the first-born. So he did not lie, but spake a truth, meaning that he was that son, to whom by divine election the first-birth-right was due, which his father supposed to pertain to Esau.

But because some scorners of Christian doctrine (like to the old Manichees) use to say, that Catholic Doctors and Schoolmen excuse and condemn whom they list by such glosses, let such reprovers understand, that both modern &amp; ancient Catholic writers avow this defence of the holy Patriarch Jacob, not by private spirit, but by the most true and proper sense of holy Scripture it-self. Where it may appear, if they will examine the text \[1\], that Jacob in all this procurement of his father's blessing, neither did any thing unjustly nor said any thing falsely. \[2\] First, it was revealed to his mother (chap. 25. v. 23.) *That the elder* (of her twins) *should serve the younger.* \[3\] Secondly, holy Scripture testifieth in the same chapter (v. 27.) *That Jacob was a plain* (or sincere) *Man,* void of unjust dealing. \[4\] Thirdly, for more quiet enjoying that right, which God had ordained for him, he procured his brother's consent and confirmation (v. 33.) \[5\] Fourthly, though he was secure in conscience that the blessing was due to him, yet he feared (v. 12.) lest he might give occasion of offence to his father, to whom this mystery was not yet revealed. \[6\] Fifthly, Isaac perceiving at last God's will, that Jacob should be preferred, was neither offended with him, nor revoked his blessing as unlawfully surprised, but condescending thereto, ratified that he had done, saying (v. 33.) *I have blessed him, and he shall be blessed.* \[7\] Sixthly, God himself from this time forwards often appeared to Jacob, and with great promises and many temporal and spiritual benefits, declared his singular love to him. \[8\] Seventhly, these three Patriarchs, Abraham, Isaac, &amp; Jacob, are special renowned Saints of the old Testament: yea the Lord and Creator of all would peculiarly be called (Exodi. 3.) the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Moyses praying instantly for God's mercy and clemency towards the people (Exodi. 32.) besought him to remember Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, his servants: and so in both old and new Testament these three are often mentioned as chief Princes in the Kingdom of Heaven. All which shew the great virtues and holiness of them all.

#### Annotation 7

**Head**

Feared exceedingly.

**Annotation Text**

\[1\] \[2\] Justly may we marvel, why Jacob, so often assured by God's promises, confirmed by his many blessings, protected in all former dangers, accompanied the night before with armies of Angels, endued also with all virtues, and namely with perfect charity (which expelleth fear) was for all this so vehemently afraid! S. Augustin answereth \[3\], that he neither distrusted in God, nor did any unlawful thing: but did his own endeavour wisely and confidently, lest by presuming or despairing he should rather have tempted God than trusted in him. \[4\] The causes of his fear were in respect of himself and his brother. For considering God's former promises, benefits, &amp; protections, were not to be presumed as absolute signs of his perpetual love, but conditional, if himself persevered sincerely in God's service; and seeing *The just man knoweth not whether he be worthy of love or of hatred* \[5\] \[6\], he might doubt, lest by his twenty years conversation among Infidels in Mesopotamia, he had contracted some sins, for which God might suffer him to fall into calamity and affliction. And though he was in deed still more and more virtuous, and consequently in God's more favour and protection; yea so much the more, by how much less he presumed of his own good state and merits: yet by the vehement apprehending of his brother's inclination to revenge \[7\], the greatness of the occasion by procuring the first-birth-right and his father's blessing from him, the news of his speedy coming towards him with four hundred men, the natural situation of the place, where Esau might easily environ him, and (as he humbly though) his own unworthiness, he was possessed with natural fear (such as happeneth to constant men) and was sore afflicted for the tender care of his family. As the Patriarch now performeth his vow to God, so God fulfilleth his promise, giving him a new name. \[1\] For *Jacob a supplanter*, signifying too small force and praise for such a champion, God therefore honoured him with the name of *Israel*. Of whom not only Moyses, but all the Old Testament most specially treateth, and of whom and in whom the promised Messias, the Redeemer of mankind, should be born.
