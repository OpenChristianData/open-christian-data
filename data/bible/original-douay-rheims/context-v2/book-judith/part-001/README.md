## Book Introduction 1

**Head**

THE ARGUMENT OF THE BOOK OF JUDITH.

**Book Introduction Text**

SAINT Jerom sometime supposed this book not to be canonical, but afterward finding that \[1\] \[2\] the *Councel of Nice accounted it in the number of holy Scriptures*, he so esteemed it, and thereupon not only translated it into Latin, out of the Chaldee tongue, wherein it was first written, but also as occasion required alleged the same as divine Scripture, and sufficient to convince matters of faith in controversy. For otherwise his opposing the authority of the Nicene Councel, should prove nothing at all against *the Jews*, seeing also they *acknowledge this book amongst Agiographa* (or holy writs) *but less fit* (say they) *to strengthen those things which come into contention*. Whereby is clear that S. Jerom thenceforth held it for divine Scripture. As further appeareth in his commentaries in Isai 14. more expressly Epist. ad Principiam, he counted it in rank with other Scriptures, whereof none doubteth, saying: *Ruth, Esther, Judith* were of so great renown, that they *gave the names to sacred volumes*. And in this Preface doubted not to say: that *the rewarder of Judith's chastity* (God himself) *gave her for imitation not only to women, but also to men: gave her such virtue that she overthrew him, whom none could overcome, and conquered the invincible*. Also Before the \[3\] Councel, *Origen* in c. 14. Iudith, *Tertullian* de Monogamia. c. ult. And diverse whom *S. Hilary* citeth, and dissenteth not from them, Prologo in Psalmos, *held this book for Canonical*. Many more writers likewise about the time of the same Councel, and after so account it. *Prudentius* in Psychomachia pudicitiae &amp; libidinis: *Chromatius* in c. 6. Mat. *Paulinus* in Natali. 10. *S. Chrysostom* hom. 10. in Math. S. Ambrose li. 3. Offic. c. 13. Epist. 82. &amp; li. de viduis. *S. Augustin* (or some other good author) writ two sermons of Judith, 228. 229. *Cassiodorus* divini lect. c. 6. *Fulgentius* Epist. 2. de statu viduarum. *Ferrandus Carthaginensis* ad Regium de re militari. *Iunilius Africanus* li. 1. de partibus divinae legis. *Sulpitius* in historia. *S. Beda* de sex aetatibus. *Alredus* writing the life of S. Edward our King. More are not necessary to reasonable men. Concerning the time, and author, it seemeth \[4\] most probable that these things happened when Manasses King of Juda was either in prison in Babylon, or newly restored to his Kingdom, who as it seemeth permitted the government to \[5\] the high Priest Eliachim (Ch. 4.) otherwise called Ioachim (ch. 15.) who also writ this Book, as \[6\] Philo's Chronology, li. 2. reporteth. From which time they had no wars till the reign of Joachaz, about 80. years, conformable to the long peace mentioned chap. 16. v. 30. In sum we have here, \[7\] not a poetical Comedy (as Martin Luther shameth not to call it, in Simposiacis, c. 29. and in his German Preface of Judith) but a sacred History (as all aforementioned esteemed it, &amp; the Jews confess) of a most valiant Matron's fact, delivering the people of God from persecution of a cruel Tyrant. The first three chapters shew the occasion of this danger: the next four describe the \[8\] difficulties &amp; distresses thereof: other seven with part of the 15. how Judith delivered them from it. In the rest Judith is much praised, and she with the whole people praise God.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 26

**Head**

After that her husband was dead.

**Annotation Text**

As young Tobias and Sara were notable patterns to married persons: \[1\] so Judith is a like good example to devout widows, excelling most part in many respects. \[2\] For first, she professed this holy state of life in the old Testament, when it was most rare, the law providing that the brother, or next kinsman, should marry the widow of him that died without children, \[3\] as it seemeth she had none, the Greek text affirming that she gave all her goods before her death to other kindred. ch. 16. v. 24. Secondly, she was only once married, ch. 15. v. 13. ch. 16. v. 26. whereas it is also commendable after twice or oftener marriage at last to abstain. Thirdly, she was young, about 36. years: for three years and a half after that her husband was dead, she was called a young maid, ch. 12. v. 12. Fourthly, she was of excellent beauty, *ch. 8. v. 7.* Fifthly, exceeding rich. *Ibidem.* Sixthly, very noble, especially after the delivery of the people from such distress. ch. 15. v. 10. Seventhly, for this renowned fact, and for her other great virtue *(ch. 8. v. 8.)* many principal men desired to marry her, ch. 16. v. 22. Eighthly, all the people wished much issue of so noble a stock. ch. 16. v. 25. Ninthly, she lived long in the state of widowhood, about threescore and nine years, from 36. to 105, ch. 16. v. 28. Tenthly, there was great and long peace in all Israel, after that she had relieved Bethulia. ch. 16. v. 30. All which might easily have invited another to have married: but her great devotion, and fervent desire to serve God in a retired austere life, fasting &amp; praying, ch. 8. v. 6. cut off all incitements to marriage, \[4\] and made her before the Gospel to embrace Evangelical counsel, not commanded, but for better attaining to perfection counseled by our Saviour and S. Paul. *Mat. 19. 1. Cor. 7.*
