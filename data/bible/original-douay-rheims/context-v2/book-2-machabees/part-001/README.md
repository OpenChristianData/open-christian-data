### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 8

**Head**

They decreed.

**Annotation Text**

\[1\] Beza in his Annotations *(in Ioan. 10. v. 22.)* set forth in English in the year 1603. confesseth that the feast which our Saviour observed, *was instituted by Judas Machabeus, and his brethren, after the restoring of God's true religion by casting out Antiochus his garrison*. \[2\] It is also clear that this feast *was in winter, ibidem*, agreeable to the text, \[3\] *in the month of Casleu*, which is November, whereas the feast of tabernacles was in September, before winter; and the feast of restoration of the temple after the captivity of Babylon, was in *Adar (1. Esd. 6.)* which is February, between which time and midst of March, was not competent space for those things which Christ did after this feast, before his Passion. And therefore it is very strange that Beza, or other Protestants will deny these Books to be Canonical: which have so excellent a testimony by the Evangelist of our Saviour's own fact.

**Annotation Subnote 2**

#### Annotation 12

**Head**

Onias prayed for all the people.\` And v. 14. \`Jeremy prayeth for the people.

**Annotation Text**

As against prayer of the faithful for the dead: *ch. 12.* \[1\] so against prayer of Saints for the militant Church, Protestants have no better evasion, when they are pressed with these examples, than by denying the authority of the Books. For seeing the Prophet Jeremy, and the High-Priest Onias, being in *Limbo patrum* (no holy soul ascending into heaven before Christ) did pray for the whole people of the Jews, it is also certain that they, and other Saints in glory, do of their excellent charity pray for those that are in this mortal life. Yet neither do we Catholics urge this place, as though we had no other to allege, for we shew the same doctrine by other holy Scriptures, \[2\] *Gen. 48. Exo 32. Jere. 15. Luc. 16. 2. Petri. 1. Apoc. 5. 6. 8.* and others: neither must we omit these books, because our adversaries deny them, seeing the learned Doctors, and holy Fathers confirm the same doctrine by those Scriptures. Among others ancient Origen, tomo 18. in Ioan, saith, it appeareth that Saints departed from this life have care of the people, as it is written (saith he) in the Acts of the Machabees, many years after the death of Jeremy: \[3\] *this is Jeremy the prophet of God, who prayeth much for the people*. Likewise S. Bernard *Ser. 3. vigil Nativit. Domini. &amp; Ser. 11. again Ser. 76. in Cantica*, admonisheth that a good religious man is like to this *Onias*: who prayeth to God for all the people.

**Annotation Subnote 2**

it is also proved by many other holy Scriptures.

**Annotation Subnote 3**
