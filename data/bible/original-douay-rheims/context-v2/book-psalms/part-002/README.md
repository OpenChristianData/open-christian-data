### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 5

**Head**

Not a God that wilt iniquity.

**Annotation Text**

Seeing *God will not iniquity* ◦, as these words testify in plain terms, it followeth necessarily, that he is not author, nor cause of any sin. For God doth nothing contrary to his own will. But he hateth iniquity, and in respect thereof *hateth all that work iniquities*, as the authors of iniquity, though he loveth them as his creatures, and of his part requireth their salvation.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 1

**Head**

For the octave.

**Annotation Text**

Literally is seemeth that the Psalms which have *For the octave* in their titles ◦, were to be sung on an instrument of eight strings. So the Chaldee paraphrasis translateth, *In citheris octo chordarum: in Citherns of eight strings*. But prophetically S. Augustine, &amp; others expound it, to pertain to the Resurrection in the end of this world. So David, and all penitent sinners bewail their sins, and do penance in this life, for the octave, that is for the world to come.

**Annotation Part**

1

**Annotation Subnote ◦**

The octave signifieth the world to come.

#### Annotation 1

**Head**

In thy fury, nor in thy wrath.

**Annotation Text**

By *fury* is signified divine justice, irrevocably condemning the wicked to eternal damnation \[1\]: by *wrath*, God's fatherly chastisement correcting sinners, whom he saveth. Whereupon S. Gregory teacheth, that the faithful soul not only feareth fury, but also wrath: because *after the death of the flesh, some are deputed to eternal torments, some pass to life through the fire of purgation* \[2\]. Which doctrine the same holy father confirmeth, by the judgement of S. Augustine more ancient. Who likewise affirmeth, that all those which have not laid Christ their foundation, are rebuked in fury, because they are tormented in eternal fire: and those which upon right foundation (of true faith in Christ) have *built wood, hay, stubble*, are chastised in wrath, because they are brought to rest of beatitude, but purged by fire. Let therefore the faithful soul (considering what she hath done, and contemplating what she shall receive) say: *Lord rebuke me not, in thy fury: nor chastise me in thy wrath*. As if she said more plainly: This only with my whole intention of heart I crave, this incessantly with all my desires I covert, that in the dreadful judgement, thou neither strike me with the reprobate, nor afflict me with those, that shall be purged in burning flames. Thus S. Gregory, *in Psalm. penitent. v. 1.*

**Annotation Part**

2

**Annotation Subnote 1**

Hell for some sinners. Purgatory for others.

**Annotation Subnote 2**

*in Psal. 37. 1. Cor. 3.*

#### Annotation 1

**Head**

Presses.

**Annotation Text**

\[1\] Most Hebrew Doctors say the word *Gittith*, may either signify the place, where this Psalm was made, or the musical instrument, on which it was sung. But most Christian Doctors expound it literally of Christ's Passion, who was stretched on the Cross, and all his sacred blood pressed, and drawn out of his body. #### Annotation 21

**Head**

---

**Annotation Text**

After the 21. verse the late Hebrew Doctors divide this Psalm \[1\], beginning there the tenth, without any new title: but only this word *Sela* \[2\]: which the Septuagint, Theodotion, and Symmachus translate *Diapsalma*, that is, change of meter, or music, also pause or rest in singing. Aquila, whom S. Jerome rather approveth, translateth *semper* ever \[3\]. Some English Bibles omit it \[4\], others leave it in the text, not translating it into English. It seemeth to most Interpreters to be added as a note to stir up attention. And it occurreth often, not only in the end of Psalms, but also in other places. For it is thrice in the third Psalm. And therefore maketh no argument, that this Psalm should be divided \[5\]. And those which divide this into two, join two in the 147. Psalm. So that all agree in the number of 150. Psalms in the whole Psalter.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 9

**Head**

The impious walk round about.

**Annotation Text**

S. Augustine expoundeth this of worldly men desiring temporal things \[1\], signified by the seven days, wherein this whole life is turned about, as in a wheel, not providing for the eighth day, which is eternity, after the day of Judgement.
