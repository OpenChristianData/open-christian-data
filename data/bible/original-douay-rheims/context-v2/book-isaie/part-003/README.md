### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 16

**Head**

Abraham hath not known us.

**Annotation Text**

The faithful people considering their own great, frequent, &amp; inveterate sins, with the extreme calamities whereinto they were fallen for the same, supposed that their progenitor Abraham (whom God had particularly called out of his country) &amp; Jacob (of whose twelve sons the whole nation was propagated) did no longer acknowledge them for their children, \[1\] because they had so grievously offended God, &amp; were not worthy of any favour. All which notwithstanding, yet they hoped in God's incomparable mercy, that his divine goodness, being Creator of all, who had elected them for his peculiar people, brought them out of Egypt, and often delivered them from sundry afflictions, would again reduce them from captivity, and as their merciful father, remit their sins, and relieve their miseries: though Abraham, Jacob, and other Patriarchs had justly rejected them as lost children. This being the proper literal sense of this place, according to S. Jerome, and other ancient Doctors' explication, it maketh nothing at all for the old and new heresy of Vigilantius, &amp; Luther, denying that Saints in another life do know what is done in this world. For albeit the Patriarchs in zeal of justice, did not acknowledge their carnal posterity (because of their great sins) for their children, yet they knew their state, \[2\] as S. Augustine *(li. de cura pro mortuis)* \[3\] interpreting this, and other places of holy Scripture, teacheth, partly by relation of such as passed from hence to them; partly by holy Angels; and especially by divine inspirations. As it is clear that Abraham knew the state of poor Lazarus, &amp; of the rich glutton, \[4\] describing what each of them had deserved, and consequently received. Much more both the old Patriarchs, and all other Saints, in eternal glory know each other, though never seen nor known before in this world, as S. Gregory teacheth: *li. 4. c. 33. Dialog*. The glorified Saints see also in God that which pertaineth to their clients, that pray unto them in earth, so far as God doth ordain, more clearly by light of glory, than Prophets see by light of prophecy: \[5\] as S. Augustine teacheth. \[6\] But touching the manner, he saith it exceeded the reach of his understanding, *how Martyrs do help those, who, it is certain, are holpen by them*. So discoursing at large of the uncertain manner, sheweth that there is no doubt at all of the thing itself, that Saints in heaven do know mortal men's necessities, hear their prayers, and help them by their intercession and merits. \[7\] Which he confirmeth also. *li. 20. c. 21. cont. Faust. Tract. 8. in Ioan. Ser. 5. de Sanctis*. Likewise, S. Jerome against Vigilantius, S. Gregory. *li. 3. Epist. ep. 30. li. 7. ep. 126. li. 9. ep. 38.* and others in many places.

**Annotation Subnote 1**

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 1

**Head**

Heaven is my seat.

**Annotation Text**

Lest any should interpret these prophetical promises (as the Jews do) only of the restoration, &amp; re-edification of Jerusalem and the Temple, the Prophet here sheweth that albeit God sanctified the temple, and granted many benefits to those that served him therein, yet his proper seat is not in any material temple or terrestrial place, but in heaven. ◦ And therefore Temples and Churches are indeed ordained for faithful people to serve him in, &amp; to signify, that as these places are more holy, than ordinary houses, so heaven is infinitely more glorious than any earthly palace: yet God is not contained in any place, but exceedeth all. To which purpose S. Steven (Act. 7.) allegeth and urgeth this place: and also S. Paul. Act. 17. that God *dwelleth not*, or is not concluded in temples, neither needeth them for his own use, but is rightly served in them, by those that lift up their minds to him, as dwelling in heaven, &amp; replenishing all places.

**Annotation Subnote ◦**

As God was served more specially in the temple, so he is now in Churches, but is in all places. *See Act. 7. v. 48.*

#### Annotation 21

**Head**

I will take of them to be Priests.

**Annotation Text**

In the Law of Moyses Priests and Levites were all of one Tribe, by succession of natural kindred, not by election; but the law being changed, necessarily also Priesthood is changed. And Bishops, Priests, Deacons, and other Clergy men, are taken, and ordained not by nearness of blood, but by election, ◦ according to their merits of virtues.

**Annotation Subnote ◦**

Clergymen in the old law succeeded by blood, in the new by election.
