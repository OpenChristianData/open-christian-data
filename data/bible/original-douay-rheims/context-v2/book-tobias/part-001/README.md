## Book Introduction 1

**Head**

THE ARGUMENT OF THE BOOK OF TOBIE.

**Book Introduction Text**

BESIDES the testimonies of Councels and Fathers before mentioned, S. Cyprian, de *Oratione* \[1\] *Dominica* alleging this book (cap. 12.) saith: *Divine Scripture instructeth us, that prayer is good with fasting and alms*. S. Ambrose (li. de Tobia. c. 1.) calleth this Book by the common name of Scripture, saying *he will briefly gather the virtues of Tobie, which the Scripture in historical manner layeth forth at large*. Where he also calleth this history Prophetical, and Tobie a Prophet. And lib. 3. Offic. cap. 14. allegeth this Book as he doth other holy Scriptures, to prove that the virtues of God's servants far excel the Moral Philosophers. S. Chrysostom ho. 15. ad Heb. allegeth Tobias as \[2\] Scripture, denouncing curse to contemners. S. Augustin made a special sermon of Tobias, as he did of Job, which is the 226. sermon *de tempore*. S. Gregory *parte 3. pastor. curae admon. 21.* allegeth it as holy Scripture. And Venerable Beda expoundeth this whole Book Mystically, as he doth other holy Scripture. *S. Jerom* translated it out of the Chaldee language, wherein it was written, \[3\] \[4\] *judging it more meet to displease the Pharisaical Jews, who reject it*, than not to satisfy the will of holy Bishops, urging to have it. Epist. ad Chromat. &amp; Heliodorum. to. 3.

The author is uncertain: but S. Athanasius (in Synopsi) reporteth the contents at large. And S. Augustin (li. quest. ex utroque testamento, 9. 119.) delivereth both the contents, and cause of writing it, briefly thus: *The servant of God holy Tobias, is given to us after the law for an* \[1\] *example, that we might know how to practise the things, which we read. And if tentations come upon us, not to depart from the fear of God, nor expect help from any other than from him.* It may be divided into three parts. The first four chapters shew the holy and sincere manner of life of old \[2\] Tobias. The eight following relate the journey and affairs of young Tobias, accompanied and directed by the Angel Raphael. In the two last chapters they praise God. And old Tobias prophesieth better state of the commonwealth.

## Book Introduction 2

**Head**

ANNOTATIONS. Concerning the Book of TOBIAS, JUDITH, WISDOM, ECCLESIASTICUS, and MACHABEES.

**Book Introduction Text**

PROTESTANTS and other Sectaries of this time deny these Books to be divine Scripture, because they are not in the Jews' Canon, nor were accepted for canonical in the primitive Church. But \[1\] indeed the chief cause is, for that some things in these Books are so manifest against their opinions, that they have no other answer, but to reject their authority: An old shift noted and refuted by S. Augustin, touching the Book of Wisdom, which some refused, pretending that it \[2\] was not canonical, but indeed because it convinced their errors. For otherwise who seeth not, that the Canon of the Church of Christ is of more authority with all true Christians, than the \[3\] Canon of the Jews? And that the Church of Christ numbereth these Books amongst others of divine and infallible authority, is evident by the testimony and definition, not only of later general Councels; of Trent, Sess. 4. and Florence *Instructione Armenorum*, of Pope Innocentius, *Epist. ad Exuperium*, and Gelasius, *Decreto de libris sacris*; but also the Councel of Carthage *An. Dom. 419.* S. Augustin *lib. 2. Doct. Christ. cap. 8.* Isidorus *lib. 6. Etymol. cap. 1.* Cassiodorus *lib. 1. Divinarum Lectionum*, Rabunus, *lib. 2. de Institutione Clericorum*, and others testify the same, as we shall further note severally of every book in their particular places. And for so much as our adversaries acknowledge these Books to be Holy, and worthy to be read in the Church, but not sufficient to prove and confirm points of faith: the studious reader may consider that the Councel of Carthage calleth them *Canonical, and Divine*, which sheweth that they are of infallible authority. For a \[4\] Canon is an assured rule and warrant of direction, whereby (saith S. Augustin lib. 11. contra Faustum cap. 5. &amp; lib. 2. contra Cresconium cap. 32.) the infirmity of our defect in knowledge is guided, and by which rule other books are likewise known to be God's word. His reason is, because we have no other assurance that the books of Moyses, the four Gospels, and other books are the true word of God, but by the Canon of the Church. Whereupon the same great Doctor uttered \[5\] that famous saying: that *he would not believe the Gospel, except the authority of the Catholic Church moved him thereunto. contra. Epist. Fundamenti. ca. 5.*

True it is that some Catholic Doctors doubted whether these Books were Canonical or no, because the Church had not then declared that they were; but since the Church's declaration no Catholic doubteth. So S. Jerom testifieth, that the Book of Judith (among the rest) seemed to him \[6\] \[7\] not canonical, till the Councel of Nice declared it to be. Likewise the Epistle to the Hebrews, the Epistle of S. James, the second of S. Peter, the second and third of S. John, S. Jude's Epistle, and the Apocalypse were sometimes doubted of, yet were afterwards declared to be Canonical. And most Protestants, namely English, admit them all, as the assured word of God though they were not always so reputed by all, but as S. Jerome affirmeth of S. James' Epistle, *Paulatim tempore* \[8\] *procedente meruit authoritatem*: By little and little in process of time merited authority.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 1

**Head**

Tarried long because of the Marriage.

**Annotation Text**

\[1\] Out of this example of a holy Marriage between young Tobias and Sara, described in the five precedent Chapters, some special good lessons may be briefly gathered, for the instruction and consolation of such as are to marry, or be already married. Not that either all, or only the same observations pertain now to christian Marriage, but that all Christians may see, how orderly godly persons proceeded in making, and performing this holy contract in the old Testament, \[2\] and thereby learn, that more perfection is required in Christian Matrimony, being now (as then it was not) a holy Sacrament, \[3\] signifying the Union between Christ and his Church: \[4\] and giving peculiar Grace to the parties (if themselves hinder it not) to bear more easily the incident burdens, \[5\] and rightly to fulfill the proper duties of man and wife, according to the godly instruction of the Roman Catechism. \[6\] All that we shall here note, may be reduced to two heads, or principal parts. For in this Marriage there were certain necessary, and as it were essential points, \[7\] without which it had not been a lawful nor right contract: Other accessory ceremonies, and Rites were also very convenient, for the more solemnity and better performance thereof. As the like of both sorts are now, but more perfect in Christian Marriages. In the former kind, \[8\] first of all, *the end of Marriage* was proposed by the Angel, and desired by the parties. *chap. 6. v. 22. c. 8. v. 9. not for pleasure but for children, and posterity, by which God might be blessed and served*: Secondly, Marriage must be *between lawful persons*: For the servants of God could not lawfully match, neither with Infidels, nor with over near kindred, but in convenient degrees, and that ordinarily in the same Tribe. *ch. 6. v. 11. ch. 7. v. 14.* as the law of Moyses prescribed. Thirdly, the *parties* must give and *express their mutual consent*. And the good will of parents was also requisite. *ch. 7. v. 10. 15. 20.* \[9\] These principal points premised; for the due solemnization other Rites were also adjoined. \[10\] \[11\] First, Raguel the maid's *father gave his daughter* taking her by the right hand, and so delivered her into the right hand of Tobias. *ch. 7. v. 15.* as with us the father, or near friend of the woman, giveth her to be married to such a man. \[12\] Secondly, her father prayed that it might well succeed, *ibidem* and so do all friends now, especially the Priest by the public prayers of the Church. \[13\] The third rite, the *covenants of Marriage* were made, with assignment of dowry, also *written ch. 7. v. 16.* &amp; sealed, as the \[14\] Hebrew and Greek text witness. \[15\] The fourth, invitation of friends to the Marriage. *ch. 8. v. 2. ch. 9. v. 3.* \[16\] The fifth, They made a *feast* which commonly dured seven days, but here fourteen *ch. 8. v. 23.* for the double joy of the marriage, &amp; expulsion of the devil. But though the feast continued long, yet was *it most moderate* and sober, *with fear of our Lord they celebrated the feast of the Marriage ch. 9. v. 12.* Plato a heathen Philosopher, *li. 6. de legibus*, prescribeth what temperance in meat &amp; drink, and what modesty of behaviour, are required in marriage feasts. Whose excellent sentence, *F. Serarius in Tob. 10.* reciteth. \[17\] The sixth rite, the bringing of the bride to her chamber, *chap. 7. v. 19.* the Catholic Church hath a particular form of blessing the bridal chamber. \[18\] The seventh, Tobias &amp; Sara observed three days continency after the Marriage ch. 6. v. 18. ch. 8. v. 4. which now is commended by way of counsel, not of precept. \[19\] The eight, Tobias observed that which the Angel advised him, in burning the liver of the fish upon coals in their chamber. ch. 6. v. 19. ch. 8. v. 2. So devout persons commonly observe that which spiritual men exhort them unto, though it be not a commandment. \[20\] The ninth, Private prayer of the new married persons, ch. 6. v. 18. c. 8. v. 4. \[21\] The tenth, the sending away of the spouse to her husband's house, or dwelling place, with good wishes, due payment of the promised dowry, and goodly admonitions by her parents. ch. 10. v. 10. 11. 12. 13.

### Chapter Annotations

#### Annotation 12

**Head**

I offered thy prayer to our Lord.

**Annotation Text**

Here the Angel Raphael reporteth certain good offices, \[1\] which he had done for Tobias. He did other like for his son, and for Raguel, and his daughter, which are likewise recorded in this Book. And the whole world, especially God's servants, receive continual great benefits by holy Angels, as partly may be gathered in this holy history, and more else-where. For first the office of Angels is, to assist, or be always ready, \[2\] as most diligent servitors of God, expecting what his divine goodness will appoint them, whither to go, and what to do for the benefit of men: as holy Raphael was sent, when young Tobias wanted a guide. Secondly, Angels offer the prayers of the faithful, or as the Greek text readeth, v. 15. Angels present the prayers of Saints, that is, of godly men and women to God: so Raphael testifieth here himself, \[3\] that he offered Tobias' prayers to our Lord. Thirdly, \[4\] Angels aid and assist those that love purity of life, sincere service of God, hate vice, embrace virtue, and do works of mercy; so Raphael assisted Tobias, when he travailed to bury the dead, flying from the King's fury, and hiding himself, ch. 1. v. 21. 23. Fourthly, \[5\] Angels exhort to good works, as in this 12. chapter, v. 6. 8. 9. 10. 18. Fifthly, \[6\] they suggest and instruct what to do, ch. 6. v. 4. 5. Raphael taught young Tobias to take the fish, unbowel him, reserve parts thereof, v. 11. 12. 13. advised him to lodge at Raguel's house, to demand Sara to wife, and v. 6. 17. instructed him, against whom devils have power. Sixthly, \[7\] they expel devils from persons, and places, ch. 8. v. 3. Raphael took and bound the devil *Asmodeus* in the desert of higher Ægypt. Seventhly, \[8\] they deliver men from dangers &amp; evils, c. 6. v. 3. as when the great fish assaulted Tobias, &amp; Sara from molestation and slander, and old Tobias from blindness. chap. 3. v. 10. ch. 11. v. 8. ch. 12. v. 14. Eighthly, when it redoundeth to the honour and more service of God, and good of the soul, \[9\] Angels procure riches, and worldly commodities, ch. 12. v. 3. Young Tobias gratefully confessed the great benefits received by his guide, concluding generally, *by him we are replenished* (saith he) *with all good things*. Ninthly, Good Angels also prove men, for their mere merit, so the Angel witnesseth ch. 12. v. 13. *Because thou wast acceptable to God, it was necessary, that tentation should prove thee*. Tenthly, and finally (for we remit the reader to larger documents of others) \[10\] after proof of patience, fortitude, and other virtues, holy Angels comfort good men; so Raphael encouraged old Tobias, saying, ch. 5. v. 13. his blindness should shortly be cured. ch. 12. v. 12. shewed him how grateful his prayers with tears and works of mercy were in God's sight. \[11\] He comforted Raguel and his family by bringing young Tobias to their house, ch. 7. v. 5. Much more, ch. 8. v. 16. both them and all Tobias his family, by driving away the devil; and lastly by revealing himself unto them. So holy Angels, \[12\] especially the proper guardian patrons of every one, are always ready to help men; guard them, exhort them to good, do instruct them, do expel evil spirits, and deliver men from many evils &amp; dangers, do procure them temporal commodities, prove their virtues, offer their prayers and good works, assist them all their lives, and at their deaths, then also bring their souls to the Judgement seat, and (if they die in good state) to eternal joy and glory, whereof the ancient fathers' writings are full. S. Gregory the great in his Dialogue, *li. 4. c. 58.* S. Athanasius *li. de communi essentia.* S. Chrysostom. *ho. 3. in Epist. ad* Coloss. li. 6. de Sacerdotio. Gregorius Turonen. *li. de gloria Martyrum &amp; Confessorum. S. Augustin Epist. ad Probam. c. 9. Epist. 68. ad fratres in eremo. li. 11. c. 31. civit. &amp; octoginta trium. qq. q. 79.* Our Saviour himself testifieth that Angels rejoice at the conversion of a sinner. Luc. 15. and therefore, they know and have care of men's states in this life, and finally Angels carried the soul of poor Lazarus into Abraham's bosom. *Luc. 16.*
