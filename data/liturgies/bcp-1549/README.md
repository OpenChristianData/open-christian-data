# Book of Common Prayer 1549: selected historical text

This private offline United States selection contains 33 public rows from a complete 34-member retained electronic expression. It is a partial source-defined witness, not a collation of a printed 1549 book. The selected rows retain original 1549 prayer-book contents from the overview page, historical prayer text, a 1550 ordinal, 1552/1559 comparative readings, and the introduction attributed in the source to Edward C. S. Gibson and identified there with the 1910 J. M. Dent edition. They also retain short factual print-variant labels. The accepted source and master preserve all 34 members privately.

The source's separately authored modern overview and combined-readings contents page, repeated site navigation and footer, six browser-image instructions, and decorative spacer figures are omitted from the selected rows with an exact reversible private receipt. Source layout attributes and private HTML bookkeeping are omitted. Named anchors, speaker-role labels, table spans, surviving source links, and 48 source-image references with their available text labels remain. These are links and labels only: no linked image or PDF payload is included.

Source credit: [Justus Anglican BCP 1549 electronic witness](http://justus.anglican.org/resources/bcp/1549/), whose pages identify Charles Wohlers as Web author and Edward C. S. Gibson as author of the 1910 introduction. The source does not establish an electronic transcriber identity or grant express reuse permission for the Web author's modern overview. The selected license is “Public domain (United States) for identified historical 1549/1550/1552/1559 prayer-book text and the 1910 Gibson introduction; modern Web-authored overview and site presentation excluded.” This is a United States historical-text qualification, not a claim that every electronic edition layer is public domain. Exact print equivalence and English Crown rights outside the United States remain unresolved. This packet does not authorize public upload or commercial redistribution.
